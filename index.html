A-200 · Leadership ledger

I run the team and still do the work.

I’m a hands-on design lead: most of my week is still design work on our apps. Alongside it, I run the team side — who we hire, what we spend, what we measure, and what leadership hears from us. Open any line.

Purpose
Design’s job is to make the next decision easier.
People
I hire for judgement, then get out of the way.
Process
Critique the work, never the person.
Proof
If we can’t tell whether it worked, it isn’t done.
01 Hiring & team building 3838 daysmedian time to hire

I grew the team in twelve months, and no one has left. I wrote the role scorecards before the job posts, designed a paid three-hour exercise that respects candidates’ time, and ran every panel on the same written rubric. Median time-to-hire: 38 days.

Hire for judgement; train for tools.

02 Budget & resourcing within 33%of plan, FY 2025–26

I own the design budget: contractors, tools and licences, research incentives and learning. My first fiscal year, October to September, landed within 3% of plan. I plan capacity in designer-weeks across the four apps first and dollars second, and rebalance it with product every quarter.

Budget in designer-weeks first, dollars second.

03 Gathering the numbers 4 × 5HEART signals gathered every month

Every month I gather a HEART scorecard for each app — happiness, engagement, adoption, retention and task success — from product analytics, support tags and a standing panel of 40 merchants. The team sees the same numbers leadership does.

I only collect numbers someone will act on.

04 Reporting up 1212 / 12monthly briefs to leadership, on time

Leadership gets one page from me every month — what shipped, what moved, what’s at risk and what I need — twelve for twelve. Each quarter I review design OKRs with the heads of product and engineering, and the asks on that page are how the design budget gets decided.

One page, every month, no surprises.

05 Design ops & rituals −3535%design-to-dev handoff time

A weekly crit, two design-review slots a week with engineering, one shared library and a written definition of ready cut the time from “design done” to “in development” by 35%. If a ritual stops producing decisions, we cancel it.

Rituals light enough to keep.

06 Roadmap partnership 4 apps, 1 planquarterly planning with product and engineering

I sit in quarterly planning with the product and engineering leads for all four apps. Design writes the problem statement before anyone writes a ticket, and every bet is sized in designer-weeks before it’s promised.

Design shows up before the tickets exist.

07 Mentoring & growth 44 levelson a career ladder we wrote together

Every designer has a bi-weekly 1:1, a written growth plan and a place on a four-level career ladder we wrote together. Designers now run crit on their own, and one took over the AI assistant’s design after I led its first release.

My job is to make myself less necessary every quarter.

Operating cadence

AI on my team

We use AI for first drafts of research synthesis, content variants and QA checklists. A designer owns every decision a merchant sees, and we grade our own AI assistant against a written rubric before every release.

A-500 · About

Hello, I’m Sara.

I trained as an architect and practised for six years: plans at 1:50, site meetings at 7 a.m., and a lot of people to keep moving in one direction.

In 2017 I moved the same craft onto screens — merchant tools, SaaS products, a design system and a virtual-care platform for patients over 60 and the people who care for them.

Today I’m a hands-on design lead in Vancouver, designing Shopify apps with my team. Most of my week is design work; I also help hire, plan the design budget, gather the numbers and report them up. At night, I paint.
